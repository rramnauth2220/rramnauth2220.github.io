01 / Models

Model Representations & Adaptation

I investigate what language models represent even when their visible outputs appear compliant, and how to make model adaptation more predictable and targeted. My methods span representation analysis, controlled behavioral experiments, and adaptation-policy selection.

Mechanistic interpretability · EMNLP 2026

The Attentional White Bear

Persistent but attenuated concept recoverability in transformer language models

Question

When a language model is instructed not to mention a concept, does avoiding that concept in the output imply that its internal representation has been suppressed?

Approach and findings

Controlled concept-suppression experiments examine how prohibited concepts remain recoverable from internal model signals, even when the model avoids producing them. The result highlights a distinction between observable behavior and internal representation that matters for interpreting alignment evaluations.

Adaptation methods · 2026 preprint

Compiling Learning Problems Into Adaptation Programs

Adaptation Compilation · available as a research preprint and Python package

Question

Can the choice of where, how, and how much to adapt a model be treated as a prediction-and-decision problem rather than as a fixed intervention policy?

Approach

I develop a typed framework for representing adaptation problems and interventions, with a compiler that predicts the effects of candidate adaptation programs before selecting among them. This line of research builds on empirical work identifying different learning signatures across transformer layers and adaptation objectives.

02 / Autonomy

AI Safety & Interactive Autonomy

Autonomous systems must decide not only what they can do, but whether acting is appropriate in context. My work explores frameworks for assistance, contextual exceptions to rules, and runtime oversight in interactive settings.

Context-sensitive decision-making

When Should an Agent Help—or Hold Back?

Question

How should an intelligent agent reason about assistance when human preferences, goals, and social circumstances make a simple “always help” rule inadequate?

Approach

I study principles for socially appropriate assistance and argue for explicitly bounded exceptions to conventional interaction rules. This agenda also motivates frameworks for runtime guardrails and oversight in deployed autonomous systems.

03 / Embodiment

Embodied AI & Human–Robot Interaction

Social robots provide a demanding testbed for autonomy: people are unpredictable, environments change, deployments last beyond a demonstration, and success requires meaningful human-centered outcomes. These projects combine system building and empirical evaluation.

Long-term deployment · RSS / HRI 2025

Autonomous Social Robots in Homes

Question

Can autonomous social robots sustain meaningful, personalized interaction beyond a laboratory session, and what changes when evaluation moves into people's homes?

Approach and evidence

My in-home research studies sustained interaction with adults and children, including personalized conversational intervention and triadic gaze behavior. These studies examine engagement, reliability, and social behavior across days and weeks—not just immediate task completion.

Real-world evaluation · RO-MAN 2025

RESET: Robot-Enhanced Social-Emotional Therapy

Question

Can an autonomous robot integrate into an existing elementary-school de-escalation room and help students return more effectively to classroom activities?

Approach and evidence

RESET was co-designed and evaluated through a month-long deployment in a school. The research examines practical integration, real-world usability, de-escalation, and return to classroom activities, with outcomes assessed in the setting where the system was intended to operate.
